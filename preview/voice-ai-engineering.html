WHEN VOICE IS THE RIGHT INTERFACE

Use conversation where it removes real friction.

Voice is valuable when speaking is faster or more natural than navigating a screen—and when the conversation can connect directly to the workflow, systems, and outcome behind it.

01The request is natural to say

Scheduling, service, status, intake, coordination, and guided workflows can often be expressed faster in conversation than through multiple screens.

02Users need to stay in motion

Voice can reduce interface friction when hands, attention, or device access are constrained during the task.

03The task spans systems

The conversation may need to retrieve context, call APIs, update records, trigger workflows, or coordinate several tools to finish the work.

04Guidance improves completion

Conversational intake and service flows can adapt questions, clarify ambiguity, and guide the user through a structured outcome.

VOICE IS NOT THE DEFAULT

Dense comparison, long-form review, private environments, or tasks that require extensive visual confirmation may be better served by screens, forms, or asynchronous workflows. Good voice engineering includes knowing when not to use voice.

FROM CONVERSATION TO COMPLETED ACTION

A tool request is not a completed outcome.

The product should move through explicit state: understand the request, establish context and authority, act through the right system, verify what actually happened, and only then confirm success to the user.

PRODUCTION PRINCIPLE

Never tell the user an action succeeded because the model expected it to. Confirmation should reflect the underlying system state—or clearly explain that the action is still pending, failed, or needs human help.

PRODUCTION STANDARDS

Real conversations create real operating requirements.

Voice systems have to work while users interrupt, change their minds, speak unclearly, wait on slow systems, fail authentication, or ask for actions the AI should not complete.

01Latency & turn-taking

Keep interaction responsive across listening, reasoning, tool use, and speaking without sacrificing accuracy or state.

02Identity & permissions

A voice interface is not proof of identity. Sensitive information and consequential actions need explicit trust and authority boundaries.

03Conversation state

Corrections, interruptions, prior choices, and workflow progress must remain coherent as the interaction evolves.

04System verification

Downstream timeouts, retries, partial failures, and stale data require explicit confirmation from the system of record.

05Human continuity

Escalation should carry the intent, gathered context, attempted actions, current system state, and reason for handoff.

06Observability & evaluation

Teams need visibility into conversation quality, action success, failure patterns, latency, escalation, and task completion.
