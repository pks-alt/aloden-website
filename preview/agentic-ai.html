WHAT GETS ENGINEERED

An agentic system is more than a model with access to tools.

A production agentic workflow has to understand what started the work, assemble the right context, maintain state across multiple steps, decide what can happen next, interact with external systems safely, recover when something fails, involve people when required, and leave enough evidence to understand what actually happened.

FROM TRIGGER TO VERIFIED OUTCOME

The workflow advances one verified state change at a time.

An agentic system should not receive a goal and disappear into an opaque loop. Each step begins from known state, makes a bounded decision, uses permitted tools, records what changed, verifies the result, and determines whether the workflow can continue, needs approval, must recover, or is actually complete.

A dependable agentic workflow does not simply keep acting. It knows when to continue, when to wait, when to recover, and when the work is actually done.
AUTONOMY & HUMAN CONTROL

Give the system enough autonomy to be useful—and no more.

The right level of autonomy depends on the action, not on what the model is technically capable of doing. Low-risk, reversible work may proceed automatically. Higher-impact actions may require confirmation, formal approval, or direct human ownership. The product should make those boundaries explicit before the workflow runs.

WHERE AGENTIC SYSTEMS CREATE VALUE

Use agentic systems when the workflow itself needs to adapt.

Not every automation needs an agent. Agentic systems create value when work spans multiple steps, changing context, several tools or systems, exceptions, approvals, and decisions where the next action cannot always be predetermined. When the path is simple and known, conventional software or workflow automation is usually better.

THE COUNTERPOINT

When an agentic system is not the answer.

Use ordinary software, deterministic automation, or a single AI capability when a simpler system can reliably solve the problem.

01

The workflow follows a known sequence.

02

Explicit rules can reliably determine the next step.

03

One model call or one tool call solves the problem.

04

There is no durable workflow state to manage.

05

Source systems cannot provide reliable verification.

06

Tool permissions and contracts are poorly defined.

07

Added autonomy does not materially improve the outcome.

Agentic systems earn their place when adapting the workflow creates more value than merely automating the steps.
