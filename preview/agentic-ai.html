WHAT GETS ENGINEERED

An agentic system is more than a model with access to tools.

A production agentic workflow has to understand what started the work, assemble the right context, maintain state across multiple steps, decide what can happen next, interact with external systems safely, recover when something fails, involve people when required, and leave enough evidence to understand what actually happened.

01GOALS, TRIGGERS & COMPLETION CRITERIA

Define what starts the workflow—and what “done” actually means.

Agentic behavior should begin from a clear event or objective rather than an open-ended instruction. Define the trigger, objective, constraints, and completion criteria so the system knows both why the work started and when the work is actually finished.

ObjectiveWhat outcome is being pursued

02CONTEXT & DURABLE STATE

Give every decision the right history.

Real workflows rarely complete in one turn. The system needs to know what has already happened, which data was gathered, which actions were attempted, what remains unresolved, and what changed since the previous step.

03REASONING, PLANNING & DECISION LOGIC

Use intelligence to choose the next useful step—not to invent the entire process.

Some workflows benefit from dynamic reasoning. Others should follow explicit business logic. The architecture deliberately separates deterministic rules, model-supported decisions, agent planning, and human judgment rather than forcing every decision through an LLM.

Deterministic RulesModel-Supported DecisionsAgent PlanningHuman Judgment

04TOOL CONTRACTS & SYSTEM INTEGRATION

An agent is only as dependable as the actions it is allowed to call.

Tools need explicit contracts defining what the tool does, what inputs it accepts, what permissions it requires, what success and failure look like, and whether the action is reversible.

FunctionInputsPermissionsSuccessFailureReversibility
Engineering areasTool schemasAPIsAuthenticationValidationRead/write boundariesIdempotencyService contracts
05ORCHESTRATION & WORKFLOW CONTROL

Coordinate the work around the intelligence.

Agentic systems often involve multiple tools, multiple decisions, parallel activity, waits on external systems, approvals, long-running processes, retries, and resumptions. The workflow layer coordinates what happens next even when the model is not actively running.

Engineering areasOrchestrationQueuesWorkflow enginesTransitionsParallelismSchedulingResumabilityTimeout handling
06PERMISSIONS, APPROVALS & HUMAN CONTROL

Capability does not equal authority.

Before the system takes consequential action, it should understand who initiated the work, what that identity can access, what the agent may decide itself, what requires confirmation or formal approval, and what must remain human-owned.

Low risk + permitted → continueConsequence requires approval → pause & request approvalRestricted / uncertain → escalate

07VERIFICATION, EXCEPTIONS & RECOVERY

Do not confuse an attempted action with a successful outcome.

After using a tool, the system should inspect what actually happened. If something fails, it should retry safely, choose an alternate path, request clarification, compensate or reverse where possible, or escalate.

Requested actionTool callSystem responseVerify resulting state
